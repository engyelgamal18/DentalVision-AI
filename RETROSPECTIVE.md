# DentalVision AI Retrospective
## What I Set Out to Do

At the beginning of this project, I wanted to understand how AI could be used with panoramic dental X-rays in a practical way.

My goal was to learn the basic workflow of an object detection project, including preparing images, creating annotations, training a model, reviewing predictions, and evaluating the results.

I also wanted to connect my dental background with the AI skills I was learning during the program.

## What Changed

At the beginning, I thought the main challenge would be training the model. As I worked on the project, I learned that preparing the data, reviewing annotations, testing the results, and presenting the project clearly were just as important.

I also became more confident working with AI tools. Instead of only asking AI for answers, I learned to use it to help me troubleshoot problems, improve my work, and understand why something was not working.

## What I Would Build Next

If I continue this project, I would like to use a larger and more diverse dataset and test the model on more unseen images.

I would also like to improve the prediction viewer so the results are easier to inspect and compare. Another next step would be to add more dental finding classes and continue improving the model evaluation.

## Three Things I Learned

The first thing I learned is that good data is very important. The model can only learn well if the images and annotations are prepared carefully.

The second thing I learned is how to test and review AI results instead of only trusting the output. I learned to look at the predictions and evaluation numbers to understand what worked and what still needed improvement.

The third thing I learned is how to use AI as a helper while still checking the work myself. AI helped me with coding, troubleshooting, and understanding some parts of the project, but I learned that I still need to review and test everything.

## How My Way of Working Changed

Before this project, I usually focused on getting the final result. During this project, I learned to work more step by step.

I started checking the data, annotations, model predictions, and evaluation results separately instead of only looking at the final output.

I also learned that when something did not work, I should test the problem and try to understand the reason instead of immediately starting again.

This made me more patient and helped me understand the project better.

## What Was Most Difficult

One of the most difficult parts was understanding how all the steps connected together. At first, I saw the data, annotations, training, and evaluation as separate tasks.

By the end of the project, I understood how each step affects the next one. This helped me see the project as one complete workflow instead of separate pieces.

## Final Reflection

When I started this project, I was still learning how AI projects are built from start to finish.

Now I understand the process much better, from preparing data and annotations to training a model, checking the predictions, and reviewing the evaluation results.

I also learned that building the model is only one part of the project. Testing, explaining the results, and presenting the work clearly are also very important.

This project made me more confident about continuing to learn AI and exploring how it can be used in dentistry and healthcare.
